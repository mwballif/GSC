\section*{Data Appendix: Sectoral Prices, Trade Costs, and Aggregation}

\subsection*{Data Sources}

\textbf{Price Levels:} Productivity Level Database (PLD) 2023, Groningen Growth and Development
Centre. Provides sectoral gross output price levels relative to the United States (USA = 1.00) for
the benchmark year 2005.

\textbf{Gross output deflators:} two World Input-Output Database (WIOD) releases: the Long-Run
release (1965--2000) and the Release 2016 socioeconomic accounts (2000--2014), spliced at 2000.

\textbf{Nominal exchange rates:} Penn World Table, local currency per U.S. dollar.

\textbf{World interest rate:} market yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury note at constant maturity,
FRED series \texttt{DGS10}, annual averages 1965--2014. Entered as the gross rate
$R_t = 1 + i_t/100$. Bonds are in zero net supply, so the same $R_t$ faces both countries and each
country's financial friction is recovered from its own Euler equation.

\subsection*{Sector Definitions}

We aggregate the underlying subsector classification to three broad sectors, mapping agriculture,
mining/manufacturing/utilities, and services to AGR, MAN, and SER respectively.

\subsection*{Construction of Sectoral Prices}

For each country $c$, sector $i$, and year $t$ we construct an internationally comparable sectoral
price level as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Extract gross output price indices from each WIOD release. The Long-Run release is
    expressed in USD and is converted to local currency by multiplying by the \emph{fixed 2005}
    nominal exchange rate $e_{c,2005}$; the Release 2016 index is already in local currency. Using a
    fixed rate here, rather than the current-year rate, ensures the year-over-year movement of the
    Long-Run deflators reflects only real relative prices and not the dollar cycle.

    \item Chain-link the two releases at 2000, taking Long-Run levels through 2000 and Release 2016
    growth rates after, then normalize to 2005 within each country-sector:
    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{\pi}_{i,c,t} = \frac{\pi_{i,c,t}}{\pi_{i,c,2005}},
    \end{equation}
    where $\tilde{\pi}_{i,c,2005} = 1$ for all countries and sectors.

    \item Extract the 2005 gross output benchmark PPP for each sector from PLD, $\text{PPP}_{i,c}$
    (local currency per USD), formed as a geometric mean of product-level PPPs weighted by gross
    output shares within the broad sector.

    \item Hold the nominal exchange rate at its 2005 value, $e_{c,2005}$, rather than the
    current-year rate.

    \item Construct the internationally comparable price level:
    \begin{equation}
        P_{i,c,t} = \tilde{\pi}_{i,c,t} \cdot \frac{\text{PPP}_{i,c}}{e_{c,2005}}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

The exchange rate is fixed at 2005 so that the level across countries reflects only real relative
prices, not the nominal dollar cycle. The same $e_{c,2005}$ converts the Long-Run deflators out of
USD before the splice, so the exchange rate never varies within a country's time series. At 2005,
$\tilde{\pi}_{i,c,2005}=1$, so
$P_{i,c,2005}=\text{PPP}_{i,c}/e_{c,2005}$ is the sectoral price level relative to the United States.
In other years the level moves only with the within-country deflator $\tilde{\pi}_{i,c,t}$. For the
United States, $\text{PPP}_{i,\text{USA}}=e_{\text{USA},2005}=1$, so $P_{i,\text{USA},t}=\tilde{\pi}_{i,\text{USA},t}$,
a pure within-country price index.

\subsection*{Trade Shares}

Bilateral sectoral trade flows come from the same two WIOD releases, spliced at 2000 on a single
matrix. Country $c$'s own domestic share $\pi_{cc,t}^{i}$ and its bilateral shares $\pi_{ch,t}^{i}$
(importing sector $i$ sourced from exporter $h$) are the row shares of that matrix. The diagonal
$\pi_{cc,t}^{i}$ is the share of home goods in home spending in sector $i$.

\subsection*{Aggregation to the United States and the Rest of the World}

The counterfactual model is solved for two countries, so all country-level data are aggregated to
the U.S. and a rest-of-world (ROW) aggregate.

\textbf{Additive quantities} (gross output, value added, expenditure, net exports), sum across ROW
members. Intra-ROW flows cancel, so the ROW's net exports are exactly the negative of the United
States's.

\textbf{Price levels} are gross-output-weighted geometric means of the member levels within each
sector and year:
\begin{equation}
    \ln P_{i,\text{ROW},t} = \sum_{c \in \text{ROW}} w_{c,t} \ln P_{i,c,t},
    \qquad
    w_{c,t} = \frac{Y_{c,t}}{\sum_{c'\in \text{ROW}} Y_{c',t}},
\end{equation}
where the weights are computed over members that have a price. This is a mean of levels, so a cheap
member lowers the aggregate price level.

\subsection*{Recovery after Aggregation}

All model variables are aggregated to the two-country system first, and the structural disturbances
are recovered from the aggregated data. In particular, the sectoral trade costs that enter the model
are recovered directly from the aggregated two-country prices and trade shares, sector by sector and
year by year. The cost paid by importer $i$ to source sector $j$ from exporter $h$ in the
two-country system is
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{ih,t}^{j} = \frac{P_{i,t}^{j}}{P_{h,t}^{j}} \left(\frac{\pi_{hh,t}^{j}}{\pi_{ih,t}^{j}}\right)^{1/\theta},
    \qquad \tau_{ii,t}^{j} = 1.
\end{equation}
By construction the ROW's own share $\pi_{\text{ROW},\text{ROW},t}^{j}$ is one minus the U.S. share of
ROW spending in sector $j$, since intra-bloc trade is domestic. No member-level parameter is pooled;
the shared block is the direct inversion of the aggregated data, which is what preserves consistency
with the two-country model.

\subsection*{Capital Stock}

The capital stock comes from the Penn World Table. The former Soviet republics enter the PWT sample
only in 1990, so the residual ROW region's capital stock jumps roughly 137\% in a single year (1989
to 1990) even though investment is flat across the seam. This is a coverage change in the source
data, not capital accumulation, and leaving it in would put a one-year spike in the recovered
investment efficiency. The residual region's capital is therefore chain-linked at 1990 the same way
the aggregates are spliced at 2000: the pre-1990 level is held through 1990, and the series resumes
with the observed capital stock growth from 1991. Every growth rate after the seam is preserved.
